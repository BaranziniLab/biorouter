Design of record · proposed 2026-07-28 · approved and mostly built

The interface, rebuilt on one set of parts.

Biorouter's design language was written down and never built. Every view re-derived it, and the audit counted the cost: five row-title treatments, four error dialects, five spinner constructions, six modal shells, 140 hand-written text-[11px] classes, and a toast surface hosting a 400-pixel scrollable report.

This page proposes the correction, and is rendered in it. Every control below is live and built from the proposed tokens — so the three switches in the bar above are not illustrations. Family proves the construction survives all three palettes. System flips the page between today's geometry and the proposal, so the diff is the page itself.

Read the switch as written, not as a mirror of the app. This was approved and mostly executed — phases 1–7, 9 and 10 have landed, phase 8 only partly. System · Today therefore means the geometry before the adoption began, which is no longer what a running Biorouter looks like; it is kept because the argument for each change is only legible against what it replaced. Where a passage describes something already built it says so and gives the file; everything else is the remaining plan, left verbatim so it stays executable. The folder README.md carries the per-phase ledger.

01

The contract

Five things are not on the table. Astryx contributes construction — one control ladder, one easing, one state model, one radius ladder — and never values. Every proposal below is written to hold these.

The palette stays

Parchment's warm neutrals, the coral accent, Alma Mater's navy and teal, Roche Limit's orange — unchanged hex for hex. Use the Family switch: the construction never moves, only the colour.

The theming pipeline stays

One authored file per family, generate-and-refuse-on-contrast-failure, the light-before-dark ordering invariant, 252 contrast assertions. The audit called it the strongest part of the system; we extend what a family may declare, not how.

The calm register stays

No bounce, no glow, no gradient, no lift on hover, no colour as decoration. Astryx agrees almost everywhere, which is what makes it a viable donor.

Corners stay square-ish

Astryx makes buttons, badges and avatars full pills. We refuse. We take the semantic ladder and the concentric-nesting rule, mapped onto squarer values; radius-full survives on status dots, the switch knob and avatars.

Chat density stays

The 760px column, tool-calls as lines (D-17), rows not cards (P2), mono for data (P6). Astryx's roomier marketing rhythm does not enter the transcript.

760 is the measure, not the floor of one. --measure-chat is a flat 760px, and the composer and the transcript both key off it so they cannot drift. It was briefly clamp(760px, 78%, 1180px), on the argument that a wide window buying margin instead of content reads as the app refusing to use the room. That is right for --measure-page, which stays fluid because a settings or sessions view genuinely buys content from the width — and wrong here: an 1180px composer is not a more capable input, it is a line the eye has to track back across, with the toolbar's controls dragged to opposite ends of the window.

Two things are on the table

The typeface (A-01) and the sidebar's vertical budget (A-08) — both because you asked for them. Everything else in §11 is a recommendation you can overturn.

02

Foundations

Six systems that everything else is composed from. Three of them (type, density, radius) change visibly when you flip the System switch.

Type scale — geometric, tokenized, on a 4px grid

Base 14px × 1.2, every line-height snapped to 4px, and semantic roles on top of the raw sizes. Today the canonical scale lives only in prose, so Tailwind's defaults stand in and the two most characteristic steps have no utility at all — hence 140 × text-[11px] and 18 × text-[13px]. Note the label role: control text is 14/500, distinct from body's 14/400. Astryx's other rule worth taking — big text goes lighter, not bolder.

Density — one control ladder

Three heights, 32 as the default for buttons, inputs, selects, icon buttons, nav rows, tabs and menu items alike. Font size does not change with height — only the box. Flip to Today and watch the ladder scatter: four bar heights, four item heights, and row actions at an off-scale 28px the Button component doesn't even define.

32 × 32, 16px glyph

Chrome: shipped one --chrome-height token at 44px, now wired: all three bands read h-chrome — the titlebar band in BioRouterSidebar/AppSidebar.tsx, the chat header in BaseChat.tsx, the artifact strip in artifacts/ArtifactViewer.tsx. It replaced 52px written three ways, with two different hairline colours and three different grounds meeting at a seam the code comments claimed was continuous. The three had to move in one edit or not at all: a band that shrinks beside a stationary one does not read as a tighter app, it reads as a misaligned one — which is why the value lives in the token rather than in any of the three files. The 8px was expected to need the sidebar compaction to pay for it; measured, it did not — the traffic lights sit in the 32px drag region above and clear a 44px band, and the wordmark is in its own row below the band, not inside it. Verified in the running app: both bands measure 44px and their bottom hairlines meet at y=44. Rows: content lists 40 → 36px (A-03 — this contradicts D-12·B; the rhythm survives, the value tightens one grid step).

Radius — semantic, and mapped square

A ladder, not a size list: inner → element → container → surface, where a container is always rounder than what sits inside it, and nesting follows inner = outer − padding. That relationship is what makes nested corners look intentional. Today --radius-lg is a silent alias of --radius-md, so 234 call sites share 8px under two names and the doc's own table disagrees with the shipped CSS.

Interaction tints — two alphas, not a palette of states

Hover and press are ink at 5% and 10%, composited over whatever surface the control happens to sit on; selection is 14%. Today every family hand-authors --sidebar-hover and --sidebar-active as literal hexes with no documented relationship to their ground, list rows use a 42% colour-mix and settings rows 38% for the identical gesture — exactly the class of derived value the theming doc says historically drifts. A tier nothing stands on is worth recording as such: it is either a consumer still to be found or two tokens to delete, and the reader should not have to grep to learn which question is open.

03

Controls

One state model, shared by every control in the system: hover is a tint, press is a tint plus scale(.98), disabled is opacity with no colour change, focus-visible is an instant outline. Because it is variant-agnostic it costs one shared class rather than a rule per variant.

Buttons

Four variants, three heights, no borders on filled variants. Destructive is a tinted danger fill with deep danger ink — not a solid red block, which is louder than anything else in a calm system. Press them.

— text stays 14px at every size; only the box changes

Retired: the shape:'pill' / 'round' props (both already render 8px — the prop is a misnomer that only changes padding), destructive's unique hover:opacity-90 (which lets the modal scrim ghost through), and the excuse for 103 raw <button> elements across 58 files.

Inputs — chrome on the wrapper

The container carries height, padding, border and radius; the native input inside is naked. That single move is what makes leading icons, trailing buttons, chips-inside-input and input groups work without per-case CSS. Focus and hover are inset rings, so nothing shifts layout — which honours "focus is a surface shift, never a ring" more literally than today's outline does. Click into them.

⌘K opens the palette
That directory does not exist. Pick another.

Field anatomy is label 14/500 muted → 4px gap → control → 12px status line: a 56px field, adopted as the Settings and forms rhythm. Error text names the fix, never apologises.

Checkbox, radio, switch

22px visual box inside a 24px hit target, 8px label gap. The switch thumb grows 16 → 20px as it slides — the one micro-delight Astryx allows itself, cheap and calm. Toggle them.

Chips and badges — the distinction, enforced

A chip is squared and carries a category or a filter. A badge is a pill and carries status. Hue chips come from one formula — hue at 24% alpha behind saturated hue ink — replacing four hand-rolled small-label recipes in the app today: 4px rounded chips, 8px rounded-md pills, a Badge component and a BuiltInBadge.

Rows — the list is the layout

Astryx's own guidance is the part to internalise: cards are not the default layout tool, and most content groups need no container at all. So a list gets no container — it sits directly on the page, separated by hairlines, and the only box that ever appears is the hover wash under the row you are pointing at. This is the one element where copying Astryx directly would break the app. Its toast is one line with an optional action, and its docs say "keep messages short: only a few words." Biorouter's toasts do not behave that way, so the census came first.

Three tiers, with a ceiling on each

A toast that cannot hold its content is not a toast. Anything with a list, a disclosure or per-item actions moves to a notification centre; a toast may link to it.

The wordmark keeps its own line. Sharing the band with the traffic lights saved 32px but buried the brand between two rows of controls; on its own line it introduces the app properly, and the compaction still lands because the row count does the work. Twenty of those pixels come back, and the rail is better for it. shipped When the band dropped 52 → 44 the wordmark came up with it and landed 8px under the hairline with the first nav row directly beneath — three elements stacked at one rhythm, which reads as crowded rather than dense. 08

The terminal

The dock claims "one tab language, both sizes", and that holds for how a tab is painted — not for how it is composed. Today it runs a 40px bar with 28px tabs, a 6px icon gap where the chat strip uses 7px, close buttons that are always visible where the chat's are hover-revealed, a cwd readout and a hide button the chat strip would never carry, and a third overflow behaviour (scroll immediately, where chat shrinks then scrolls then overflows to a menu).

One strip language, bottom edge included

The dock keeps its own furniture — a working directory belongs there — but adopts the shared tab. The one thing it stops inheriting is the sidebar ground: --sidebar was chosen so the chat strip could continue the top edge, and at the bottom edge that rationale inverts, leaving the dock reading as a floating slice of sidebar. It grounds on the surface with a hairline instead.

How running reads
  • The leading glyph carries the breathing ring — an expanding, fading ring on a 1.8s period, the same motif as the thinking dot above it and the sidebar's running dot. That ring is the entire signal.
  • One motion, not two. A sweeping progress bar along the row was tried and cut: with the ring already breathing, the row had two things moving for one fact. Movement in the transcript always means "still going", and it should say so once.
  • Elapsed time counts up in the right slot, and only appears after 2s — a tool that returns instantly should not flash a timer.
  • The live detail (· compiling biorouter-mcp) is the one place the row changes text while open; it replaces itself rather than appending, so the row never grows.
How finishing reads
  • The ring stops and fades over --dur-fast — it does not finish its cycle first. Motion ending is the completion signal.
  • The glyph swaps to a check in --text-success and the elapsed time freezes, gaining the result summary beside it.
  • Nothing moves after that. A finished row is completely still, which is what makes a running one legible three rows away.
  • Stopped is not pending. It takes its own glyph and says who stopped it, so an interrupted call can never be mistaken for one that never began.
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion the ring holds still at 45% opacity: the state still reads, the movement goes.

This is additive — the collapsed row, its verb grammar and the humanised summaries are all kept. What it adds is a state that the eye can find without reading: today, distinguishing a running call from a finished one means parsing the first word of the line.

Motion

Astryx runs everything on one easing — cubic-bezier(0.24, 1, 0.4, 1) — and nine durations. The governing rule is visible in the data: the bigger the element, the slower the motion. Entrances are staged; exits get out of the way.

The problem is not the tokens, it is the default

Biorouter's three-duration scale is correct and loses anyway: Tailwind's own 150ms and cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1) were never re-pointed at it, so every bare transition-colors silently runs a fourth duration and a fourth curve. The count is 49 tokenized sites against ~74 literal durations and 150+ bare utilities.

TierValueApplies to
fast-min95msmenu-item hover, row hover, table rows — the highest-frequency feedback
fast125msbuttons, switches, inputs, chips — control state changes
fast-max175msmenus, tooltips, tab indicator
medium300mssidebar collapse, panel resize, accordion, progress fill
medium-max450msdialogs, command palette
slow-min525msambient loops only — the spinner

One recipe for every overlay: fade + a small move toward the final position (8px menus, 16px dialogs) + scale(.95), fill-mode backwards. Route changes stay instant — and the eleven page-transition classes that decorate views while being defined in no stylesheet get deleted rather than implemented. Reduced motion means instant state change, not no state change: spinners slow to 3s/turn rather than freezing, so necessary feedback survives.

Decisions

Everything above is a recommendation. These ten are where a different answer changes the work.

A-01

Typeface — bundle the advertised face, or keep the native stack?

The app renders in whatever the OS provides, so it has no typographic voice. Astryx advertises one geometric-humanist sans for everything. Inter is already bundled for the wordmark, so the mechanism and the licence question are both settled.

Bundle it. ~35KB subset, and one re-run of the contrast script at the new metrics.

A-02

Type scale — adopt the 14-base geometric scale with semantic roles?

The canonical scale currently has no tokens at all, so Tailwind's defaults stand in and ~180 arbitrary pixel classes fill the gaps.

Yes. The largest cheap win in the audit, and the first time the documented scale becomes enforceable.

A-03

Content rows 40 → 36px, contradicting D-12·B

D-12·B fixed 40px as "one rhythm, no compact mode". Astryx's dense-data band is 32–40.

Tighten. The principle survives; only the value moves, and the app gains about 10% more rows per screen.

A-04

Radius — composer and modals 16 → 12px; full restricted to dots, knob, avatars

Flip the System switch at the top of this page to see it both ways.

Yes. Keeping 16px anywhere but the artifact sheet reintroduces the fork this is meant to close.

A-05

Interaction tints replace the hand-authored per-family hover/active hexes

Every family authors those values by hand today with no documented relationship to their ground.

Yes. Removes the exact drift class the theming doc warns about, and shrinks what a new family must author.

A-06

Selection — achromatic wash and weight, with one accent mark per surface

Astryx drops accent colour from selection entirely so the accent stays reserved for CTAs. You asked to keep the colour, and the straightened rail already shipped in this worktree.

Adopt the wash, keep the rail. Selection then reads even where colour is not the signal.

A-07

Toast tiering — a notification centre for compound reports

The grouped extension report is a 400px interactive panel living in a transient layer. Nothing else in the census needs more than three lines and two buttons.

Yes. Split it out; let the toast link to it.

A-08

Sidebar compaction — wordmark into the band, "MENU" deleted, Scheduler and Workflows grouped, Applications and Apps merged

Returns roughly 130px of rail and drops the item count from nine to seven.

Yes to all four — the nav grouping is what users will notice, so it is the one most worth iterating on if you disagree.

A-09

Focus — keep the surface shift, add an instant 2px outline for :focus-visible only

These are separable: pointer focus keeps the surface shift, keyboard focus gets a real indicator. Today keyboard focus is nearly invisible on several controls.

Yes.

A-10

Scope — all ten phases, or a subset?

Phases 1–3 are pure infrastructure; they are what make the rest small.

All ten, in order. Stopping after 5 would leave two systems running at once — which is the state the app is already in.
