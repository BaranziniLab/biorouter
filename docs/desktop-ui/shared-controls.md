# The shared controls

> **What this is.** The numbers and rules the desktop app's shared controls are built to (switch, segmented control, the one rule for hover and click, InfoTip, the composer footer's cards, setting row, row actions, menus, tooltips, fields, tabs, dialogs and sheets), and the traps each one has already hit.
> **Status:** Current
> **Audience:** developers working on the desktop renderer, and agents editing `ui/desktop/src/components/ui/`

Every screen in the app draws its controls from `ui/desktop/src/components/ui/`, and the CSS that cannot be left to utility classes lives in `ui/desktop/src/styles/main.css`, in the blocks marked `@ws WS-PRIMITIVES`. The reasons for the numbers are in section 2.6 of [the redesign spec](../design/codex-simplicity-redesign/implementation-spec.md). This page is for the person who has to change one of them: what the control promises, what pins it, and what went wrong the last time.

## One control for each job

| Job | Control | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A change that applies now | `Switch` | 32 by 20, a 16px knob inset 2px in both states, on the right of its row. Named by the row's visible label, never "Toggle …". |
| Picking items, or an option sent with a form | `Checkbox` | A 16px box in a 24px hit target, the label 8px to its right. |
| 2 to 4 short values that need no explanation | `SegmentedControl` | A radiogroup. One thumb slides under the choice and never uses the accent. The thumb is a step lighter than its track in both modes (`--background-default` in light, `--background-strong` in dark, where the default ground is darker than the track and the choice would read as a hole). A change that Radix and the arrow-key handling both ask for is passed to `onValueChange` once; that guard lasts only for the synchronous burst of the two asks (it is reset in a microtask), so a parent that ignores a change does not swallow the next press of the same segment. |
| 4 or more values, or values that each need a line | `Select` or a menu | The chosen row carries a trailing 16px check. |
| Sections of a page | `Tabs` | One underline that travels under the active tab. |
| An explanation | `InfoTip` | See below. |
| A filter that opens a menu or a popover | `FacetChip` (`ui/facet-chip.tsx`) | The Knowledge graph's facet chip, shared with the decision history's Source, Outcome and Filters chips, so the two cannot drift into two copies of one long class string. A `button` meant for `DropdownMenuTrigger asChild` or `PopoverTrigger asChild`; `engaged` writes `data-engaged="true"`, which `.br-facet` in `main.css` paints with the engaged fill, and an engaged chip names its value ("Source: Monitors"). `ui/facet-chip.test.tsx` pins the class string. |
| A link to the decision history | `DecisionHistoryLink` (`decisions/DecisionHistoryLink.tsx`) | One look: the `Button` link variant with `.br-decision-link`, accent ink, no underline at rest, never centred, never filled, on the last line of its block. Its accessible name says what it opens and its label tooltip repeats it ("Open this decide call in the decision history"). With children it is the quiet line form (`.br-decision-link--line`, default ink, accent on hover and focus), the turn marker's source lines. In a dialog footer or a menu the same destination is a button or a menu item named "Decision history" instead. `decisions/DecisionHistoryLink.test.tsx` fails if another file in the folder writes the link text itself. |

## Hover and click

One rule answers the pointer everywhere in the app: **hover peeks, click pins** (the owner's choice, 2026-11). A hover shows a surface for a quick look, and it goes when the pointer moves off. A click keeps the same surface open so its buttons can be used, and the trigger looks pressed while it is kept. [design.md](../../design.md) D-43 records the decision; this section is the rule as the code holds it.

- **H1. A hover only shows.** A pointer hover shows words or a preview. It never acts, changes a value or a selection, moves focus, opens a menu or a modal dialog, or closes or replaces something a click opened. Touch has no hover, so a tap is a click. A focus set by script never opens anything (`isFocusFromTabKey` in `ui/Tooltip.tsx`). A Tab onto a disclosure trigger previews it the way a hover does, and focus that leaves a kept surface closes it.
- **H2. A click commits.** There are two kinds of trigger.
  - An **action trigger** (a button, a row, a tab, a link, the trigger of a menu or a popover) does its job on the click. Its label hint closes on the press, never pins, and never comes back over the menu or popover it opened.
  - A **disclosure trigger** only shows a surface: the InfoTip glyph, an InfoTip `asChild` badge (or line, such as a Monitors dialog row's settings line), the footer's folder, context ring and cost, and a Chat summary roster row, whose peek holds "Open in a tab". A click, a tap, Enter or Space keeps the very surface the hover previewed, and a second click closes it. Enter or Space on a surface that holds controls also moves focus into it. A press or focus inside a preview keeps it.
- **H3. A disclosure shows one surface.** Its hover and its click show the same surface, never two. The inverse hint surface (a tooltip, an InfoTip) holds plain text and no control. The light card (the footer cards) holds readings and controls.
- **H4. Only a click leaves a mark.** A preview leaves its trigger at rest, so the one mark that outlives the pointer means "this stays until you close it". The states are in the table below.
- **H5. The delays are a ladder**, and this pass changed none of them (table below). Footer cards have no skip window.
- **H6. Closing.** A preview closes on pointer leave (after its grace), a Tab away or a blur, Escape, and a scroll of an ancestor of its trigger. A kept surface closes on Escape, a press outside, a second click on its trigger, a click, Enter, Space or Tab onto a sibling in its group, or focus that really moves elsewhere in the page (Radix's focus-outside dismissal). It never closes on pointer leave, a scroll, a window switch or a hover over a sibling. A group shows one surface at a time, and while a footer card is kept a hover on another footer trigger opens nothing.
- **H7. One modifier click.** The folder chip's ⌘-click on macOS, Ctrl-click on Windows and Linux, opens the folder in the file manager, in the desktop app only. It is the only modifier click the interface defines. In a `biorouter serve` browser there is no file manager to open, so the same click is a plain click and keeps the card.

### The delay ladder

| Surface | Opens after | Closes after | Constants |
|---|---|---|---|
| InfoTip | 200ms | a 100ms grace | `INFO_TIP_OPEN_DELAY_MS`, `INFO_TIP_CLOSE_GRACE_MS` (`ui/info-tip.tsx`) |
| Footer card, and a Chat summary roster row's peek | 300ms | a 150ms grace, re-armed while the pointer moves along its way to the card | `FOOTER_CARD_OPEN_DELAY_MS`, `FOOTER_CARD_CLOSE_GRACE_MS` (`bottom_menu/useFooterCard.ts`) |
| Label tooltip (`Tooltip`, and a `title=` that `AppTooltipLayer` draws) | 500ms, or at once within 300ms of another one closing | at once | `TOOLTIP_DELAY_MS`, `TOOLTIP_SKIP_DELAY_MS` (`ui/Tooltip.tsx`) |
| A sidebar chat row's hover card | 700ms | at once | `CHAT_HOVER_CARD_DELAY_MS` (`BioRouterSidebar/RecentChats.tsx`) |

`AppTooltipLayer` imports the two label-tooltip constants rather than declaring its own 500, and it keeps the same 300ms skip window the Radix label tooltips have: while a title tooltip shows, and for 300ms after it closes however it closed, the next one opens with no delay.

### A trigger's states

| State | Ground and ink | Marked by |
|---|---|---|
| Rest | transparent, `--text-muted` | nothing |
| The pointer on it | `--overlay-hover`, `--text-default`, whether or not a preview shows yet | `:hover` |
| A preview open, the pointer elsewhere (on its way to the surface, or in it) | the rest look, or the focus look if Tab opened it | `aria-expanded="true"` on a footer trigger, which paints nothing |
| Kept | `--background-fill-strong`, `--text-default`, held whether or not the pointer is on it, so its rule comes after the hover rule | `data-pinned="true"`, with `aria-expanded="true"`; never `aria-pressed`, because the trigger is a disclosure and not a toggle |
| A press | `--overlay-pressed` at once | `:active`, the last rule |
| Keyboard focus | D-15's focus surface, which wins over every state above | `:focus-visible` |
| Forced colours | every fill is repainted as Canvas, so a kept trigger also draws a 1px `CanvasText` outline at `outline-offset: -1px` | `@media (forced-colors: active)` |

The kept step is subtle. Off the hover overlay it is about 1.09:1 in light and 1.08:1 in dark, and off the page at rest about 1.21:1 and 1.23:1 (Parchment; the InfoTip glyph measures 1.10:1 and 1.08:1 off hover). What says "kept" is mostly the behaviour, the surface staying put; the fill is the trace the click leaves on its trigger. The surface looks the same whether it is a preview or kept.

An action trigger whose menu or popover is open wears the same kept step: the Tools chip and the model chip (`.br-picker-chip[aria-expanded='true']`, `bottom_menu/pickers.css`), the tab strip's overflow ▾ (`.br-tabstrip__overflow[aria-expanded='true']`, `main.css`), the artifact preview's `⋯` (`.br-preview-icon-button[aria-haspopup][aria-expanded='true']`, `artifacts/preview-panel.css`), and the ghost triggers that carry `.br-surface-trigger` (`main.css`): the composer's `+`, a row's `⋯` in `RowActions` and in History, and the sidebar's chat-row and folder `⋯`. Those four rested clear and muted under their open menus while the Tools chip beside the `+` held its fill; a sidebar row whose menu is open is also lit, and its `⋯` is one step past the lit row. The chips and the ▾ hover on `--overlay-hover` and press on `--overlay-pressed`, and the `⋯` keeps the ghost button's own hover; the open fill holds whether or not the pointer is on the trigger. None of them draws the forced-colours edge, because an open menu shows on its own. "Open" is always `aria-expanded`, never `data-state`: a trigger inside a `Tooltip` gets the tooltip's own `data-state` merged over the menu's, so `[data-state='open']` never matched on the `⋯` (measured in Chromium). The preview's fold toggle also carries `aria-expanded`, which is why its `⋯` rule asks for `aria-haspopup` too.

### A label hint on a menu or popover trigger

A label `Tooltip` around a non-modal menu or popover trigger came back over the surface it had opened: a non-modal surface leaves pointer events on the page, so a pointer that left the open trigger and returned fired the tooltip's `pointermove` again, and 500ms later the trigger's name sat over its own menu, where Escape then closed the hint instead of the menu. `useSurfaceTriggerHint(surfaceOpen)` (`ui/surfaceTriggerHint.ts`) is the fix, and the Tools chip, the model chip, the composer's `+`, `RowActions`' `⋯` and a History row's `⋯` use it (History draws its own `⋯`, because its menu is the shared chat-row menu rather than a `RowActions` item list, so it missed the first fix). The artifact preview's `⋯` (`PreviewMoreMenu`, `artifacts/PreviewBar.tsx`) keeps the same rules through its panel tooltip, `PreviewTip`, given `surfaceOpen`; it showed "More actions" over its menu's first item until then:

- It refuses the open at the trigger, by cancelling `pointermove`, never inside `onOpenChange`. Radix tells the shared provider a tooltip opened before it calls `onOpenChange`, so a root that ignores the `true` leaves the provider's skip flag set and the next tooltip anywhere opens with no delay.
- It refuses a focus the same way: one that a script moved (Escape on the menu hands focus back to its trigger), and any focus while the surface is open. `Tooltip`'s Tab gate declined that open too, but inside `onOpenChange`, so after Escape on the model chip or the `+` the next label tooltip anywhere opened in 12ms instead of 500. A menu or popover trigger does nothing on focus, so cancelling its focus event skips only the tooltip. A Tab onto a closed trigger still shows the hint.
- A press holds the hint shut until the pointer leaves the trigger, so a click that closes the surface does not hand the trigger its name back under a pointer that has not moved.
- A surface that opens while the hint shows (Enter, Space or ArrowDown on a trigger Tab reached, or the app opening the Tools popover itself) closes it for good, and closes it through Radix: the hook leaves `open` as the hint set it and sends Radix's own "a tooltip opened" event, on which a showing tooltip closes itself. Hiding it by flipping `open` to `false` first left Radix nothing to close, so the provider never heard that a tooltip closed and the next label tooltip anywhere opened at once. A DropdownMenu opens on the key's keydown with no click after it, so the model chip, the `+` and a row's `⋯` all did this from the keyboard.
- The `Tooltip` stays controlled from its first render to its last (#50), which is why the `+` menu and the model chip's menu are now controlled too.

A sidebar chat row's hover card does the same job its own way (`!rowMenuShown` in `RecentChats.tsx`).

### Not under the rule yet

- A script focus on any other `Tooltip` trigger (a plain button that a closing dialog hands focus back to, say) is still refused by the Tab gate inside `onOpenChange`, after Radix has told the provider a tooltip opened, so the provider's skip flag stays set and the next tooltip anywhere opens at once. The menu and popover triggers above refuse it at the trigger instead (`useSurfaceTriggerHint`), but `ui/Tooltip.tsx` cannot cancel focus for every trigger, because a tab or a roving item acts on its own focus, so the general case waits on a decision.
- There is no InfoTip group: a hover over a second InfoTip while one is kept still previews it.

## InfoTip

An InfoTip is a visible 14px glyph after a label, and a disclosure trigger under the rule above. A hover opens a preview after 200ms and a Tab opens one at once; a preview closes on pointer leave (with a 100ms grace), blur, Escape and a scroll of an ancestor, and leaves the glyph unmarked. A click, a tap, Enter or Space keeps the very tip the hover previewed, and so does a press on the preview itself; a second click closes it. A kept tip carries `data-pinned="true"` on its trigger and closes only on Escape, a press outside, that second click, or focus that really moves elsewhere in the page (Radix's focus-outside dismissal). A pointer leaving, a scroll and a window switch, which blurs the trigger with nowhere for focus to go, leave it open.

The glyph's states are drawn on its 20px mark box (`.br-info-tip::before`, `main.css`): the hover overlay under the pointer, `--background-fill-strong` while kept, the pressed overlay on a press, each with `--text-default`; keyboard focus (D-15) wins. The trigger's `data-state` still says whether the tip shows, but nothing paints it any more, so a preview looks like rest. Forced colours draw a kept glyph's 1px `CanvasText` outline inside the mark box.

The words are always in the page: a visually hidden node holds them and the glyph points at it with `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader hears the help when it reaches the control without any hover. `useInfoTipId()` lets the explained control point at the same node.

- Never inside a `<label>` and never inside a row whose click toggles something. It sits next to the label, and a click on it stops there.
- Never `title=`. The tooltip layer copies a title into `aria-label` and renames the control.
- Never the only place an error, a refusal, a privacy disclosure or the consequence of a destructive action is written.
- The `asChild` form makes an existing badge the trigger. The target carries `biorouter-focus-region` so the base focus rule (a fill and an inset edge for every `[tabindex]`) stays off it and its outline is the only mark. Enter and Space keep or close its tip, because a span with a tab stop gets no click from a key; a target that is itself a native control keeps its own keys.
- An `asChild` target's own fill and ink are its meaning (a status tone, Private's ink against Public's, a neutral badge that already rests on the strong fill), so no state repaints them. Each state lays an ink wash over the target and 2px past it instead (`.br-info-tip-target`, an inset shadow plus a spread): the hover overlay under the pointer, the pressed overlay while kept and on a press, about 1.21:1 off the page in light and 1.31:1 in dark, so a kept badge reads as held down. Keyboard focus removes the wash so its outline is the one mark. In forced colours a kept target's `CanvasText` outline sits 1px outside it, not inside, because the 12px padlock has no padding to give up.
- There is no `aria-expanded` on an InfoTip: its surface is `aria-hidden` and the words reach assistive technology through `aria-describedby`.
- Inside a menu, Tab leaves the menu (Q2-50) and Radix's Up and Down visit rows only, so a menu that holds an InfoTip puts it in that order: the model chip's menu passes `moveFocusAcrossMenuStops` (`bottom_menu/EffortSection.tsx`) to its content's `onKeyDownCapture`, and Up and Down then take its "About this model" glyph, the dense affiliation badge, the "About effort" glyph and the selected effort segment in reading order between the rows. A focus moved by an arrow key is a script's, so it previews nothing; Enter or Space keeps the tip, and Escape closes a kept tip and leaves the menu open (`bottom_menu/effortSection.browser.test.tsx`).
- The privacy padlock (`PrivacyBadge`'s dense form) and both `AffiliationBadge` forms explain themselves through an `asChild` InfoTip with their words unchanged (the privacy copy is frozen), not a `title=` on a span no key could reach. Inside another control (a button, a link, a label, a menu or listbox row, a tab, a toggle, a field or another InfoTip's trigger: `INFO_TIP_HOST_CONTROL_SELECTOR`) they draw no InfoTip and leave the words to the control that holds them (`useInsideAControl`, the pattern `BuiltInBadge` uses), because a tab stop inside a button is a control inside a control. So the padlock in the composer's model chip has no tip of its own: the chip's name and hint already carry the tier.
- Its name is "About" and the label's words. When the label is a node rather than a string, the words it shows are read from it (`plainTextOf`), and `Field` takes an explicit `infoLabel`. A field id is never a name: the workflow editor's "Initial prompt (optional)" was announced "About workflow-prompt" (V3-R3-3).
- Its focus mark is a 20px box 2px inside the 24px hit box (`::before`), not the hit box itself. The hit box starts 1px before the label's end, so a mark on it lay over the label's last letter (V4-R3-1). The OS ring under `prefers-contrast: more` and forced colours is drawn on the same 20px edge.

## Composer footer cards

The three items on the footer line under the composer (the working folder, the context ring and the cost, which in a chat also holds the chat's usage limit) each open a card, and the three are one control. `bottom_menu/useFooterCard.ts` holds the behaviour and `bottom_menu/FooterCard.tsx` the format. The consumers are `DirSwitcher.tsx` (with `FolderAccessCard.tsx`), `ContextWindowIndicator.tsx` and `CostTracker.tsx`. The Chat summary's subagent roster uses the same hook and group for its rows' peeks (`summary/SummarySubagents.tsx`), drawn in a `PopoverContent` with the card's class to the left of its row, because `FooterCard` itself always opens above its trigger. `ComposerFooter.tsx` wraps the line in `FooterCardGroup`, which carries the composer card's ref and keeps one card open at a time; a component rendered without the group still works alone. A new footer item uses the hook and the card, never a `Tooltip`: the three used to be a hover card, a one-line dark tooltip with a separate click popover, and a multi-line dark tooltip, and each answered a click, a key and a hover differently.

The card is the light popover surface `PopoverContent` draws (`biorouter-popover-surface`: the default ground, a hairline, a shadow and a 12px radius), at most 320px wide, with 12px of padding and its sections 12px apart. It is built only from `FooterCardSection` (a muted 12px label over its content), `FooterCardValue` (the 13/18 reading in the default ink), `FooterCardPath` (a folder, below), `FooterCardDetail` (a muted 12px line under a value), `FooterCardList` and `FooterCardRow` (rows with an optional trailing action, such as Revoke), `FooterCardNote` (a muted 12px sentence) and `FooterCardActions` (secondary `xs` buttons, `FooterCardAction`, whose label is set at the card's 13/18 reading size by `.br-footer-card__action` rather than the control role's 14px, which made a button the largest text in the card), so the same kind of reading looks the same in all three. A row's action centres on the first line of the row's value, `margin-top: calc((var(--text-secondary--line-height) - var(--control-compact)) / 2)` (-3px for an 18px line and a 24px button), and still clears the section label above it. A track inside the card (the context card's bar) rests one step deeper than the card: `.br-footer-card` sets `--br-track-ground` to `--background-fill-strong`, which a dark popover re-points to the page's well. While the surface itself holds keyboard focus its edge turns `--border-focus`. The CSS is `.br-footer-card*` in `bottom_menu/pickers.css`, authored rather than Tailwind strings.

The cost card's detail under the total lists every token bucket the chat's ledger recorded, in Settings > App > Usage's order, so on the ledger its figures add up to the summary rail's Tokens and to Usage's billed total: "22,788 input · 382 output · 65,408 cache read". A cache bucket that holds nothing, or was not recorded, says nothing, and a cost priced from the live counters (no ledger rows yet) names no cache bucket, because the counters do not split it out. Input, output and one cache bucket fit the card's 294px column on one line; with both cache read and cache write the cache takes a second line, still inside the one muted paragraph. Each figure keeps its word and its "·" with no-break spaces, so a line can only break after a "·" (`COST_CARD_COPY` in `bottom_menu/copy.ts`). A model's row under By model reads the same way, after its cost and with "in" and "out".

### The cost card's Limit section

In a chat the cost card ends with a **Limit** section (`bottom_menu/UsageLimitSection.tsx`), the one place a person sets this chat's usage limit. The daemon's rules for limits are in [Usage limits](../agent-loop/usage-limits.md); this is what the card shows and writes.

- **What it reads.** The limit in force (`$5.00`, `500,000 tokens`, both, or "No limit"), where it comes from ("Set for this chat", "Default from Settings", "No limit for this chat" or "No default limit is set"), what the chat has used against it, and the month's line when a monthly limit is set. Amounts are in the shared dollar format (`utils/formatUsd.ts`), and a used amount beside its limit never reads alike unless it is (`$0.177 of $0.18 used`). A paused chat gets a danger note saying which limit holds it, or that the next request would pass it (`pausedByEstimate`) with a line giving the estimate and what is left; at 80% a muted note says it is near. A dollar limit that cannot fire because nothing is priced says so.
- **What it writes.** Set limit… (Change limit… once the chat has its own) opens one field and a Dollars | Tokens `SegmentedControl`, with Show costs on or off; a priced model starts in dollars, an unpriced one in tokens, and choosing dollars for it says a dollar limit cannot stop this chat. The field's accessible name says its unit ("Limit for this chat, in dollars"). "No limit" lifts every per-chat limit for this chat and "Use default" returns it to the default; each shows only where it would change something. Every write is `PUT /sessions/{id}/usage-limit` through the generated client with `userActionHeaders()`, and the answer replaces what the card shows. The field takes `5`, `$5`, `0.013`, `250,000`, `250k` or `1.5m` (`parseLimitInput`, `components/usage/usageLimits.ts`); a dollar amount is kept as typed, to four decimals, and a fifth is refused rather than rounded; anything else marks it invalid and names the problem. Enter saves, Escape closes the card, and when the pressed control goes away focus moves to the card surface.
- **Where it cannot write.** A `biorouter serve` browser has no proof of a person (SD-8), and a subagent's chat counts toward its parent's limit, so both say so in place of the controls. A chat with no id yet (`#/pair` before its first message) says a limit can be set once it starts.
- **The trigger is always there.** With a limit controller (`ChatInput` passes one in every chat), the cost item stays on the line with no cost worth showing, in a new chat and with Show costs off: it is then the gauge glyph alone (`data-testid="chat-usage"`, named "Usage and limit"), and with Show costs off the card shows no dollar spend, though it still shows and offers a dollar limit. It is the same element as the cost figure, so a kept card stays kept when the first cost arrives. `data-limit-state="warn"` tints its ink `--text-warning` at 80% and `"paused"` tints it `--text-danger`, and its name says so.
- **When it reads.** `useSessionUsageLimit` (`bottom_menu/useSessionUsageLimit.ts`) is owned by `ChatInput`, which reads the limit when the chat opens, after every turn, whenever the chat's recorded usage moves (a title's side call lands after the turn) and when a stored limit notice lands (`conversation/usageLimitTranscript.ts`); a composer with no chat yet reads the monthly state from `GET /usage/summary` and holds Send while the monthly limit is reached; the card reads again each time it opens; Settings > App > Usage announces its own saves on `window` (`USAGE_LIMITS_CHANGED_EVENT`). While the daemon says the chat is paused, the composer shows one `.br-composer-notice` line ("This chat reached its usage limit." or "This chat's next request would pass its usage limit." with Change limit, or "The monthly usage limit is reached." with Open usage settings) and Send refuses by button and by Enter. Change limit keeps the cost card open and moves focus into it. The stored notice in the transcript is the ordinary quiet inline line; it never pauses the composer by itself, because it stays after the limit is lifted.
- **Its figures.** A model row's cost is the daemon's per-request price (`ModelUsageRow.cost`, `costIsPartial`), which carries the cache rate and the long-context tier, when the row has one; otherwise the card prices the row with the flat table as before (`serverPricedCost` in `hooks/useCostTracking.ts`).

Tests: `bottom_menu/CostTracker.usageLimit.test.tsx`, `ChatInput.usageLimit.test.tsx`, `settings/usage/UsageLimitsRows.test.tsx`, `components/usage/usageLimits.test.ts`, `utils/formatUsd.test.ts`.

### A folder in the card

A folder is a name with a place ([design.md](../../design.md) D-42), so the card writes it as a reading, not as a machine string. `FooterCardPath` is a `FooterCardValue` with the class `br-footer-card__path`: 13/18, `--text-default`, the card's own sans, one ink for the whole path, and never truncated, because the card is the one place the whole location is read. Nothing in `bottom_menu` is mono (`styles/popupType.test.ts` allows none: `MONO_ALLOWED` is empty), and `FooterCardValue` has no `mono` prop any more.

- **What it says.** `tildePath(path, homeFolder())` from `utils/folderPath.ts`: `~` for the home folder, `~/Desktop/BioRouter` under it, and the absolute path anywhere else, with the separators the daemon wrote (`~\Desktop\x` on Windows). `homeFolder()` answers only for the desktop's own managed daemon (`BIOROUTER_LOCAL_BACKEND`, the test `utils/folderAccessLifetime.ts` makes). In a `biorouter serve` browser, or a desktop pointed at an external backend, the folder may be on another machine, where `~` would name the wrong home, so the path stays absolute.
- **Its markup.** A `<p dir="ltr" data-path="<absolute path>">` drawn by `PathSegments` (`ui/path-segments.tsx`), the same markup as the transcript's folder-access approval: each name in its own `<bdi>` and the separators outside them, so a right-to-left name cannot reorder the separators around it. Each name and the separator after it sit in one `<span class="br-path-piece">`, and a leading separator joins the first name (`/Volumes/`, `Lab/`, `data`).
- **How it wraps.** It breaks only after a separator, because each piece is one atomic box no wider than the line (`.br-footer-card__path .br-path-piece`: `display: inline-block; max-width: 100%`) and a line breaks only between two boxes. Drawn as plain text, as it first was, a path took no break after a backslash before a letter nor after "/" before a digit, and it broke after a hyphen and at a space inside a name (`biorouter-` then `workflows-and-skills`, `single cell ` then `atlas`) where a break at the separator before it would have fitted; a box around the name alone let a separator start the next line. All measured in Chromium (`footerCard.browser.test.tsx`). Only a name wider than the line breaks inside its piece, at its own spaces and hyphens first, and `overflow-wrap: anywhere` stays the last resort, for one with none (a content hash). There is no `word-break: break-all`, which made every letter a break point.
- **What a selection copies.** Exactly what is drawn, `~` and all (`~/Projects/kdps`), however many lines it takes: neither a piece nor a `<bdi>` adds a character. A triple-click is the exception the browser makes: Chromium selects the paragraph with the break after it, so its copy read `~/Projects/kdps` and two newlines, and pasted after `cd ` in a terminal it ran the line. A copy that starts in the path drops the line breaks at the selection's two ends (`copyWithoutEdgeBreaks` in `FooterCard.tsx`); a selection with none is left to the browser.

The working folder and every grant row use it. `utils/folderPath.ts` is the renderer's one rule for writing a folder (`utils/pathUtils.ts` imports node modules, so it cannot be); `normalizeFolder` and `tildePath` moved there from `BioRouterSidebar/sidebarChatView.ts`, which re-exports them, and `ui/path-segments.tsx` (`PathSegments`) draws the same markup for the transcript's folder approval card and the terminal dock.

### How each item behaves

Every item is a disclosure trigger under [Hover and click](#hover-and-click): a hover or a Tab opens a **preview**, and a click, a tap, Enter, Space, or a press or focus inside the card **keeps** it.

1. A mouse or pen resting on the trigger opens a preview of its card after 300ms (`FOOTER_CARD_OPEN_DELAY_MS`). Touch never opens one by hover.
2. Leaving both the trigger and the card closes a preview after 150ms (`FOOTER_CARD_CLOSE_GRACE_MS`). A pointer moving through the grace polygon toward the other surface keeps it open, and so does entering the card.
3. A Tab onto the trigger opens a preview at once. A focus set by script does not. Tab away closes it. The cards are portalled to the end of the page, so where nothing tabbable follows the line (the folder chip on Home, a readout that ends a chat's line) the next Tab lands in the card instead, and focus that enters a card keeps it, so a pointer crossing the trigger can no longer close it with focus inside.
4. A click or tap on the trigger keeps the card, the same card a hover previewed, and a second one closes it. Enter or Space keeps it and also moves focus onto the card surface (a named dialog), so the next Tab reaches its first control. In a card that holds no control (a cost card without its Limit section's buttons, a locked chat's folder card with no grants) that Tab closes the card and carries on from the trigger to whatever follows it.
5. A press inside the card keeps it and moves focus into it, onto the control pressed or onto the card surface, as the browser's own focus for a press does (no focus edge). The context gauge's bar and marker keep that focus too: their drag no longer cancels the press, and `user-select: none` on `.br-context-gauge` stops the drag from selecting text.
6. Escape closes the card and returns focus to the trigger, and so does Shift+Tab off the card surface. A press outside closes it and leaves focus where the press put it.
7. One card is open at a time across the line, and a hover never replaces a kept card. A card that opens closes the other open one, without moving focus, unless that one is kept: while a card is kept, a hover on another trigger opens nothing, and neither does a hover that was already waiting when the card was kept. The kept card, the focus in it and anything typed there stay as they are. A click, a tap, Enter or Space on another trigger, or a Tab onto it (which opens that card as a preview), still replaces the kept card, and a hover still replaces a preview. The group (`FooterCardGroup`) asks each card through `keptElsewhere`, which reads a copy of the card's state written in the same call that sets it, not in an effect, so a hover in the same task as the click that kept a card cannot arm against it.
8. A kept card survives the trigger's blur when the window loses focus; a preview closes on blur. Focus that really moves elsewhere in the page still closes a kept card, through Radix's focus-outside dismissal. Coming back, the window refocuses whatever had focus, which is why rule 5 matters: a press that left focus in the draft read as focus outside the card and closed it.
9. A scroll of an ancestor of the trigger closes a preview. A scroll inside the card or in a sibling scroller does not, and no scroll closes a kept card.
10. The trigger shows its card's state. It is a real `<button type="button">` with its own `aria-label`, `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, `aria-expanded` (true for a preview and a kept card alike, never `aria-pressed`) and, while open, `aria-controls`. Only a kept card marks it: `data-pinned="true"` on the trigger and on the surface, and `button.br-footline__item[data-pinned]` paints `--background-fill-strong` with `--text-default`, after the hover rule so it holds under the pointer. A preview leaves the trigger at rest, and the card looks the same either way. In forced colours a kept trigger draws a 1px `CanvasText` outline inside its box. The context ring's track is authored (`.br-footline__ring-track`, `--border-subtle`), and a kept ring's track takes `--border-strong`: on the strong fill the resting ink measured 1.06:1 in light and 1.05:1 in dark and the ring lost its circle, where `--border-strong` gives 1.26:1 and 1.29:1, about the step it has at rest. `check-contrast.mjs` holds that pair in every family and mode.
11. The card hangs above the composer card, never over the draft or its controls, and below the 44px drag band (a top collision padding of 52px). The folder card aligns to its chip's left edge, the context and cost cards to their trigger's right edge.

`bottom_menu/footerCards.contract.test.tsx` holds the three items to these rules: it renders the real `ComposerFooter` and runs one table of cases over the folder, the context and the cost, so a rule that holds for one item and not another fails there. Section 10 checks that a hover or Tab preview marks nothing, that every way of keeping (a click, a tap, Enter, Space, a click on a preview, a press inside one, focus entering one) marks both ends and holds after the pointer leaves, and that every way of closing drops the mark. Section 7 runs every ordered pair of items: a hover on the second opens nothing while the first is kept or holds focus, nor when it was waiting before Enter kept the first, nor in a `serve` browser while the folder card holds a typed approval key, which keeps its value and its focus; a click, Enter, Space or Tab on the second still replaces the first. `bottom_menu/useFooterCard.test.tsx` holds the group's kept rule on its own. `bottom_menu/footerCard.browser.test.tsx` measures the same three in Chromium, where the placement, the pointer's way into a card, the state ladder and the surface's look can be read, and checks that the three cards' padding, radius, border, shadow and type compute the same; its "folder card's folders" block reads the path's face (the label's, not mono, 13/18), what a selection copies, where long slash, backslash and digit-led paths break, that a right-to-left name keeps its order, and where Revoke sits. `bottom_menu/footerCardTrack.browser.test.tsx` measures the context card's empty track in every family and mode. A new item joins these tables.

## Rows and row actions

A setting row is 40px at minimum with the label, an optional InfoTip, an optional status line and one control. Whatever the control's height (a 20px switch, a 28px select, a 32px field), a one-line row stays 40px: the control reaches into the row's padding with a negative margin.

In a `SettingRow` the value and the control sit on the label's line, not on the row's middle. A status line ("Restart to apply", "Checking for updates…") grows the row downward, and nothing in the row moves: centred on the grown row, the switch a person had just clicked slid 8px down under the pointer (V4-R3-5). A hand-built row with a permanent second line, such as a local model's facts, is not a `SettingRow` and keeps its control centred.

The divider is a straight line drawn with `::after` and inset by the row radius. A bottom border on a rounded box curls up at both ends, and forced colours show it plainly.

A list whose rows read apart by air and the hover wash drops the divider with `.br-rows-flat` on its shell or any ancestor (R-27: "row lists drop inter-row hairlines where spacing and the hover fill already separate rows"). Only the `::after` goes, so no row moves. Forced colours repaint the wash as Canvas, so the divider comes back there, and the last row still has none. The divider stays the default because it is Crew's recipe (`styles/crewParity.test.ts`); Workflows, the Scheduler, Built apps and the Settings dialogs opt out.

A washed row (under the pointer, or holding keyboard focus) is a fill, so its controls step back one ground, as they do in a filled card: a secondary button or a Select in it rests on the page ground and hovers on the strong step, and a neutral progress track in it steps one in. The wash is the page ink at 5%, which lands on the rest fill's own value in light, so without this a button lost its box the moment the pointer reached its row. A neutral badge steps back with them: it rests on the strong step, which the wash lands 1.10:1 off in light and 1.08:1 in dark, so a "Built-in" or a change's kind read as bare ink under the pointer (V3-R2-5). The badge paints through `.br-badge-neutral`, which reads `--br-badge-ground`, and the washed row sets that to the page ground (1.10:1 light, 1.14:1 dark off the wash). The privacy pills (`PrivacyBadge`, `AffiliationBadge`) rest on `--background-muted`, the rest fill's value, and take the badge's ground on a washed list row, so one row is one ground; not on a settings row, where Crew draws its one-piece "Private · UCSF" chip.

A row washes and firms its line for keyboard focus only: `:focus-visible` on the row, or `:has(:focus-visible)` inside it. It was `:focus-within`, which is true for a switch the person has just clicked, so the row stayed washed and its line dark until focus moved. A click on a switch leaves nothing; Tab, an arrow key and a click into a text field still show the treatment. A row's `disabled` disables its control and dims only the row's words, because the control already dims itself to 50% and the two together put a locked switch at 25%.

`RowActions` shows its buttons when the row is hovered, holds focus, or has its menu open, and always under `(hover: none)`. Only opacity changes, so the buttons stay in the tab order. The optional `meta` (a relative time) shares the buttons' grid cell and fades out as they fade in. It must keep `pointer-events: none`: below opacity 1 it becomes a stacking context that paints above the opacity-1 buttons, and the invisible time ate the click on the `⋯` button.

## Menus

One row for the dropdown, the context menu, the command list and the Select options: 32px at minimum, 12px of padding, 13px text, a highlight of `--overlay-hover`. A selected checkbox or radio item shows a 16px check at the trailing edge, with no gutter on the left. A group label is 12px, sentence case, muted.

A menu opens with opacity, a scale from .97 and a 4px move toward its trigger, from the trigger's own transform origin, over 175ms. It closes with opacity alone over 125ms. The Select menu is not a Radix surface, so it plays the same values as a keyframe (`.br-select-menu`).

After a pointer pick the menu does not hand focus back to its trigger. Radix does that after the picked item unmounts, and Chromium then reads it as keyboard focus and draws the focus surface on the trigger. A key pick, Escape and a Tab out still return focus.

A dropdown menu opened with the pointer rings no row. The trigger opens on a cancelled `pointerdown`, so Radix's script focus on each row the pointer crosses read as keyboard focus and the row wore the 2px inset ring. The menu surface records how it is driven, `data-menu-input="pointer"` or `"key"` (from `ui/inputTrail.ts` when it opens, then from the keys and presses inside it), and a pointer menu's rows keep only their hover highlight. The first key pressed in the menu brings the ring back; a menu opened with Enter, Space or an arrow keeps it; `prefers-contrast: more` and forced colours keep their outline. A right-click context menu never had the problem and is unchanged.

## Motion

Layout snaps and content glides, so a control moves only when the person chose something.

- The segmented thumb and the tab underline are measured with a `ResizeObserver`. A measurement the observer takes (a resize, a tab added before the active one, a label changing) lands at rest, and both carry `data-motion-layout`, which the resize rule uses to stop them while the window or the sidebar is being dragged.
- The first placement never animates. Until it is measured the selected segment paints its own ground.
- The underline is measured in the list's own pixels. A dialog pops in at scale .98, and `getBoundingClientRect` inside it is scaled, so the unscaled width of the list is used to undo that. Measured raw, the underline stayed about 2% off after the dialog settled.
- A switch whose value arrives without a touch (it loaded, or another window changed it) snaps. Its commit carries `data-motion-still`.
- Every duration is a `--dur-*` token and the curve is `--ease-out`. Reduced motion is handled by the global reset and by explicit rests for the loops.

## Focus

Focus is a surface shift plus an inset 2px edge, never a ring. Four rules keep it from appearing when a mouse did the work:

- A tooltip (`Tooltip`, `AppTooltipLayer`, `InfoTip`) opens on focus only when Tab moved the focus, so a dialog or menu handing focus back never pops it.
- A menu skips its focus return after a pointer pick, as above.
- A list or settings row takes its wash and firmed line from `:focus-visible`, never from `:focus-within` (see Rows and row actions).
- `DialogContent` and `SheetContent` give focus back to the control that opened them. Radix returns focus to a dialog's own `Trigger` and to nothing else, so a dialog opened from state left focus on the page body after Escape. The opener is read in `onOpenAutoFocus`, because the wrapper is mounted while the dialog is still closed. A caller's own `onCloseAutoFocus` runs first and wins when it cancels the event (Crew's dialogs do), and an opener that is gone is left alone. Whether the returned focus shows a ring is the browser's call from how the person closed it: a click on Cancel shows none, Escape does.
- A dialog whose field takes React's `autoFocus` has focus inside before Radix's focus scope mounts, so Radix never fires `onOpenAutoFocus` and the opener was never read: Escape left focus on the page body (WS-SKILLS-2). The content reads its opener when its ref attaches instead, as the control focus left to reach the field (`focusArrivedFrom` in `inputTrail.ts`), resolved like any other opener, so a menu pick still returns to the menu's trigger. `ui/dialogAutoFocusReturn.browser.test.tsx` drives it; Radix's own content leaving the body is its instrument.
- A dialog opened from a menu item follows the menus' own pointer rule. The item is gone by the time the dialog closes, so the opener is the control that opened the menu: a DropdownMenu names its trigger (`aria-labelledby`, or `aria-controls` when a caller's own `id` replaced the one Radix uses), a submenu climbs to the root trigger, and a ContextMenu, which names nothing, returns to the control that held focus when it opened (`inputTrail.ts`). How the dialog is closed decides, as it does for the menu itself. A close with a key (Escape, or Enter or Space on Cancel) gives focus back, so ⋯, Enter, Enter on "View info", Escape lands on the ⋯, and so does a ⋯ clicked open, an item clicked and Escape: that path used to leave focus on `<body>`, and the next Tab began at the top of the window (V1-R8-1). A close with the pointer gives nothing back, because a script-focused ⋯ would wear the ring after a mouse click and `:focus-within` would pin a row's hover actions. One exception: a ContextMenu's opener is only the control that last held focus, and a right-click on a part of a row that takes no focus moves none, so after a pointer pick it is a guess (a search field, another row) and nothing is given back; a row that knows which of its controls opened the menu passes its own `onCloseAutoFocus` (`chats/menuFocusReturn.ts` `returnFocusAfterKeyClose`). A pick made with the pointer opens quietly: after a pointer pick every script focus matches `:focus-visible` in Chromium, so a Cancel button or a link wore the ring after a mouse click. Focus goes to the dialog box, which draws none (`.biorouter-modal-surface:focus`), unless the dialog opens on a text field, which is ringed in every modality anyway, or its caller cancels `onOpenAutoFocus` and places focus itself. `ModalShell` and `ConfirmationModal` both pass `onOpenAutoFocus` and `onCloseAutoFocus` through. `ui/dialogFromMenu.browser.test.tsx` drives it with real keys and mouse.
- Any pointer open is a quiet open, from a menu or not (V4-R5-1). A click on a trigger that already holds keyboard focus (Escape gave it back, ringed, and the mouse then opened the dialog again) changes no focus, so Chromium keeps the keyboard modality and Radix's auto-focus inherited the ring: Reset data's "Select all", Configuration's "Go to Privacy", Usage's InfoTip. So after a pointer press, `DialogContent` and `SheetContent` put focus on the box unless the first stop (the control Radix would focus: the first tabbable, links left out) is a text field. The shared `Select`'s typing input does not count as one, because it reads as a closed control. A key open keeps Radix's first stop, ringed, and a caller that cancels `onOpenAutoFocus` still places focus itself. `ui/dialogPointerOpen.browser.test.tsx` drives it; its instrument is Radix's own content ringing its first button on the same reopen.

## Dialog placement

`ModalShell` pins every dialog's top edge at `max(10vh, 48px)` and draws the header hairline, on any surface, because Crew's dialogs are the reference design. A dialog whose height changes while it is open (a tab, a result replacing a form, an error) grows downward instead of re-centring under the pointer. A dialog that reads wrong pinned to the top passes `anchor="center"`, with a one-line reason beside it, and one that wants no hairline passes `headerRule={false}`. A `ModalShellDefaultsContext` still overrides both for an area, and a prop beats the context. `ModalShell.test.tsx` pins the defaults, the opt-outs and the precedence.

An InfoTip beside a dialog's title goes in `titleInfo`, never inside `title`. The dialog is named by its heading, and an InfoTip inside the heading would add its "About …" name and its hidden help to the dialog's name. "Report a problem" (`ui/Diagnostics.tsx`) was on a raw `DialogContent`: centred, no header rule, a 36px warning tile before the title and 28px footer buttons (V4-R4-2). It is now `ModalShell` md like the rest, its footer is three word-only default buttons (with their glyphs they wrapped at 480px under Large text), and it opens with focus on the dialog box, because Radix's first tabbable there is the title's InfoTip. `ui/diagnosticsDialog.browser.test.tsx` measures it in Chromium.

The typed confirmation `ui/DangerousConfirmDialog.tsx` is a `ModalShell` sm too, like `ConfirmationModal` (V3-R7-1): "Make "Soul" public?" sat centred at y=262 in a 900px window with 24px insets and no header rule, while every other dialog opened from the Knowledge band sat at y=90. Its title is in the header, its message is the description in body text, and its focus rule is unchanged (Cancel after a key open, the dialog box after any other). Inside Crew it takes `CrewDialogs`' defaults, as `ConfirmationModal` does. `ui/dangerousConfirmGeometry.browser.test.tsx` measures it against `ConfirmationModal` in Chromium, light and dark. `ui/WorkflowWarningModal.tsx` moved onto `ModalShell` lg with a scrolling body in the same pass. Every dialog is a `ModalShell` now. The last raw `DialogContent` on a path a person can reach was the non-private model disclosure (`privacy/NonPrivateModelDisclosure.tsx`), which moved under coordinator ruling 23: `lg`, `purpose="required"` (no ×, Escape and a backdrop press do nothing, so "I understand" is the only way out), focus on the prose, and its "not saved" notice in the footer above the button, outside the scrolling body. `privacy/NonPrivateModelDisclosure.required.test.tsx` fails the day its purpose stops being `required`, and `privacy/nonPrivateModelDisclosure.browser.test.tsx` measures it in Chromium. The one exception is the unmounted, frozen `settings/tunnel/TunnelSection.tsx`; the guard also checks that nothing imports it.

## A Button inside a Radix trigger

`Button` writes `data-slot="button"` after the props it receives. A Button wrapped by `<DropdownMenuTrigger asChild>`, `TooltipTrigger`, `DialogTrigger` or any other trigger is handed that trigger's own `data-slot` through Radix's Slot, and a slot written before the spread lost to it. The element was drawn as a button but no longer matched `[data-slot='button']`, which is where `main.css` puts the forced-colours edge, the link underline and the copy field's footer, so a filled trigger had no boundary in Windows High Contrast (about sixty sites). Nothing keys on a trigger's slot, so a Button is always a button. `button.test.tsx` pins the order of the two.

## A Button that cannot act

Colour on a button means "this will do something", so a button that cannot is not coloured (vision V4-R7-4, coordinator ruling 22). A disabled `default` (coral) or `destructive` (danger) `Button` draws what a disabled `secondary` draws: the control ground, the default ink and the 50% fade `Button` already gives every disabled variant. The two variants carry the class `.br-button-inert` (`button.tsx`); the rule is authored in `main.css` beside `.br-button-xs`, unlayered, because the variants' own `bg-*` and `text-*` utilities sit in the `utilities` layer and would beat a layered rule. Its ground is `--br-control-ground`, the variable a secondary rests on, so the neutral button steps back to the page inside a filled card and forward on a recessed ground exactly as the Cancel beside it does.

Before it the app had two looks for "not yet". The provider dialogs draw a secondary until their fields are filled (first-run FR-R3-3: Configure's Save, Add provider's Create provider, Detect provider, Versa's Connect), while Tool permissions' "Save changes", Make workflow's "Create and run" and Reset data's "Reset" drew a pale coral or a faded danger wash. The two rules are one rule with two doors:

- **It cannot be pressed.** Set `disabled`. The primitive draws it neutral; no dialog needs a line for it.
- **It can be pressed and has something to say** (a click names the field that is missing, a browser hears the host-managed refusal). Pass `variant="secondary"` until it can act, as FR-R3-3 does, and leave it enabled.

A button that is WORKING and says so with `aria-busy="true"` keeps its colour: the composer's Send is disabled while a chat starts and has no label to say "Sending", only a spinner, so it stays the accent. A labelled button that is disabled while it works goes neutral like any other disabled button, and its label ("Saving…", "Resetting…") carries the progress. `aria-disabled="true"` is covered like `disabled`, for the buttons that fade without the attribute because a disabled button would hand its focus to `<body>`. The other variants are untouched: a disabled `ghost` stays transparent and a disabled `outline` is the secondary box it always was. Forced colours repaint the fill and the ink, so the system button edge every `[data-slot='button']` takes is all the rule leaves them.

## Emphasis names its weight

A bare `<strong>` or `<b>` is 700: Tailwind's preflight gives both `font-weight: bolder`, and the type system draws 400 and 600 only (vision V4-R7-6). Arial draws 600 and 700 alike, so no screenshot shows it; only the computed weight does. Every emphasis in a shared control or dialog carries `font-semibold` (or `font-normal`, for a strong that must not stand out): the workflow warning's "Title" and "Description", the update dialog's "Restart & update", and Report a problem's two warning leads. Each other owner did the same for theirs (privacy, Settings, Biorouter Copilot). `ui/emphasisWeight.sourceGuard.test.ts` fails on a bare one in any component under `components/` (coordinator ruling 24, chosen over a `b, strong` base rule in `main.css`). It reads the syntax tree, so a tag in a comment or a string is not counted, and `font-medium` passes too (Arial draws 500 as 400). The only exclusion is `settings/tunnel/`, frozen and imported by nothing, which the guard also checks. `settings/dictation/`, the inherited cloud dictation, was excluded too until PR-01 of the [dictation plan](../dictation/implementation-plan.md) deleted it in 2026-11.

| Button `secondary`, and `outline` (now the same box) | `--br-control-ground` (`.br-fill-rest`) | `--background-fill-strong` (`.br-fill-hover`), with the 0.98 press | none; `outline` keeps a transparent 1px border so nothing that used it reflows |
| `Badge` `neutral` | `--background-fill-strong` | | none |
| `Card`, a neutral `Note`, `.biorouter-modal-panel` and `-row` | `--background-fill` | | 1px, transparent |
| `EmptyState`'s mark tile (`.br-empty-tile`) | `--br-control-ground` | | 1px, transparent |
| `SegmentedControl` track | `--br-track-ground`, else `--background-fill` (the value `--background-muted` had) | | none; the thumb stays the raised surface |
| `Progress` neutral track (`.br-progress-track`) | `--br-track-ground`, else `--background-muted` | | none |
| `CopyField` | `--br-control-ground`, else `--background-well` | | 1px, transparent |

`--br-control-ground` is a private variable, `--background-fill` unless something sets it. A fill never sits on a fill: a `Card`, every `Note`, a dialog's inner panel and row, and any filled box that adds the class `br-fill-surface` set it to `--background-default` for their children, so a field or a secondary button inside rests one step back on the page ground and needs no class of its own. It is a private variable because the shared fill tokens are declared once per mode and nowhere else (`styles/themeNeutrals.test.ts`). Inside a dark popover the strong step is re-pointed one step deeper than the popover ground, which is why the filled button steps between two tokens instead of taking `tint-interactive`'s ink overlay: an overlay would lighten it into the ground.

The neutral badge rests on the strong step, not the rest fill, because a badge sits on rows that wash on hover and the hover wash has the rest fill's value. On the rest fill a "Built-in" badge vanished into its row the moment the pointer arrived. While its row is washed it steps back to the page ground (see Rows). The fill is the authored class `.br-badge-neutral`, layered in `components`, so a caller's own `bg-*` still wins and no ground needs a Tailwind class to have been scanned.

Every edge that is kept transparent is still a border, never `border-0`: forced colours repaint the fill as Canvas and draw a transparent border in the system ink, so the box keeps its outline there, and nothing moves when an edge appears. A field is not found by its fill alone: its label or placeholder and its focus edge carry it, which is the trade-off design.md 9.11 records.

Three private variables say what a ground asks of the primitives on it, and each primitive reads them with its old value as the fallback, so a plain page draws what it drew:

| Variable | Read by | Filled box (`Card`, `Note`, modal panel and row, `.br-fill-surface`, a washed row) | Recessed ground (`.br-ground-recessed`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--br-control-ground` | fields, the Select trigger, `secondary` and `outline` buttons, `CopyField`, `EmptyState`'s tile | `--background-default`, one step back | `--background-fill-strong`, one step in |
| `--br-control-hover` | a filled button's hover and press (`.br-fill-hover`) | `--background-fill-strong` | `--background-strong`, one deeper again |
| `--br-track-ground` | the segmented track, a neutral progress track | `--background-fill-strong`, one step in | `--background-fill-strong` |

A track goes one step in rather than back because a raised or coloured mark rides it: a white track would hide the white segmented thumb. A field's hover stays on the strong step on a recessed ground, where it equals the field's rest, because no deeper neutral keeps the placeholder at 4.5:1.

A recessed ground is one where the rest fill is the ground itself: the sidebar (1.03:1), `--background-muted` (1.00:1: first run, the Knowledge graph canvas, the terminal dock) and the page band. Put `.br-ground-recessed` on the ground; a filled box inside it sets its own grounds again for its children. `ui/groundsAndRows.browser.test.tsx` measures every cell of the table in light and dark.

Crew is not converted. Its own recipes (`crew-*` classes) keep their lines; the shared primitives it renders take the fill like everywhere else.

A `Note`'s action is centred on the note's first line (`.br-note-action`): a 28px row pulled in by half its difference from the 12/16 line, so a one-line note with a Retry is as tall as one without (36px) and the words sit level with the button instead of 6px above its centre.

Below about 1600 wide the 760px reading column reaches under the toast layer's top-right corner, so the trailing actions of a notice at the top of a page sit under any toast standing there. The toast cannot step around them, because everything near it is page content (below the notice is the list's first switch, beside its actions is its error). So the actions step aside instead: a group marked `data-toast-aside` moves left while a toast stands beside it, out from under the toast and 16px clear of it, and never closer than 8px to the element before it, the title its row names. `components/alerts/toastClearance.ts` measures it and main.css ("toast clears the corner") applies it as a `translate`, so nothing else on the page moves or rewraps, and it never moves from under a resting pointer. A `Note`'s own action slot runs the note's full height and cannot see the title, so a notice that needs this lays its title, actions and error out in its own grid. The Extensions page's load-failure notice is the one that does (final smoke, 2026-10-12); `alerts/toastAside.browser.test.tsx` measures it in Chromium.

Inside a dark popover the ground is lifted to `--background-medium`, so the hairlines inside it are lifted too: `--border-subtle` and `--border-default` point at `--border-strong` there. On the lifted ground the old separator measured 1.05:1 and vanished; `--border-strong` gives back the 1.29:1 it had on the page.

## Fields and the cascade

The field's fill and edges, and the defaults of a textarea (three rows, vertical resize), sit in `@layer components`. That matters in both directions. A layered rule lets a caller's `border-border-danger`, `bg-*`, `min-h-*` or `resize-none` win, as a caller expects. An unlayered rule outranks every utility whatever its specificity: the first version of the edge hid the red border four forms pass in, and the first `.br-textarea` ignored `resize-none` and kept the workflow editors at three rows. A caller's `bg-*` on a filled control also beats the layered hover, so a control given its own ground needs its own hover. The `Field` wrapper is `.br-form-field`, not `.br-field`, which belongs to the input alone.

The Select's trigger is react-select's, which moves focus to a hidden input, so `.br-field` cannot reach it: its fill, its hover and its edges are written in the styles API, as Emotion's unlayered rules.

react-select draws its own filled chevron and cross, which read heavier than every other glyph. The Select replaces both with the app's 16px stroke 1.5 icons (`ChevronDown`, `X`) through its `components` prop, ahead of whatever a caller passes, and the clear mark is muted until the pointer is over it.

react-select writes its own `display: block` on an option in an unlayered style sheet, which beats the row's `flex`. The Select sets `display: flex` in its styles API so the label and the check share a line.

Escape with a Select's menu open closes the menu and nothing else. A react-select menu is not a Radix layer, so inside a dialog the dialog is the top layer, and Radix listens for Escape in the capture phase on the document, before react-select sees the key: one Escape used to close Switch models with the provider menu still open. The Select claims that Escape first, from a window capture listener, when the key's target is its own combobox (`.br-select`) and the combobox says `aria-expanded="true"`. It cancels the event, which Radix and every other surface leave alone, and react-select closes the menu. The class scopes the claim, because cmdk's input says expanded for as long as it is mounted. A caller that passes its own `onKeyDown` would stop react-select closing the menu on that cancelled key; none does.

A pick leaves focus on the Select, as a Radix control gives focus back to its trigger. The wrapper used to turn react-select's `blurInputOnSelect` on for every caller, so a pick blurred the field, and inside a dialog Radix's focus trap moved the lost focus onto the dialog box, which shows no ring. In New schedule, ArrowDown and Enter on "AM or PM" picked AM and left focus nowhere a person could see, and the next Tab went back to Name (V3-R8-2). The blur is now off unless a caller passes `blurInputOnSelect`, and none does: the trigger keeps its focus edge and Tab goes on to the next control.

A group heading is drawn only for a group that has a name. react-select draws a `GroupHeading` for every group, and an unlabeled one is an empty 8px row above the first option: Switch models' Model field passes its options as one unlabeled group, so its menu opened with the first row 13px below the surface's top against the Provider menu's 5px (the 1px border and the 4px list padding) in the same dialog (V4-R8-1). The Select now returns no heading for an empty or blank label, and a caller's own `GroupHeading` still replaces it. `ui/selectGroupHeading.browser.test.tsx` measures a flat list, one unlabeled group (portalled, with the model picker's two-line option label) and named groups in Chromium; its instrument puts the library's heading back and reads the 13px. `ui/Select.groupHeading.test.tsx` is the jsdom half.

A field that holds a machine string says so with `<Input machine />`: a command, a URL, a model id, a region, an environment variable or header name, a config value (V4-R7-5). Chromium underlines any word its dictionary does not know, and a correct `claude` or `us-west-2` drawn with a red dotted line reads as a mistake the person made. `machine` sets `spellCheck={false}`, `autoCorrect="off"` and `autoCapitalize="off"` ahead of the caller's own props, so one field can still opt back in. The same trio is exported as `machineStringProps` for the one place that builds a props table before the element exists (the provider form's field list; `settings/config/machineString.ts` re-exports it, so there is one spelling). A field that holds a person's own words (a name, a description, a message) never takes it, and a secret field needs nothing because `SecretInput` already switches spellcheck off. `ui/input.test.tsx` pins the markup; the engine's side is read in Chromium by `customProviderForm.browser.test.tsx` and `capabilities/settingsBFill.browser.test.tsx`.

The filter field's tooltip ("Filter · ⌘F") is a hover hint for a field the person is not in. It never opens on focus, Tab included, or while the field holds focus, and focus closes it: it used to sit under History's band while the person typed. There is none when the placeholder already names the shortcut (History's "Search history… ⌘F") or when ⌘F is off. The shortcut reaches assistive tech as `aria-keyshortcuts`.

## How these are tested

jsdom lays nothing out and runs no Tailwind, so a component test can only read class names. The acceptance numbers are measured in Chromium:

- `ui/controlsGeometry.browser.test.tsx` bundles the real components with esbuild, styles them with the authored blocks of `main.css`, and measures the switch, the menu row, the segmented thumb, the InfoTip, the tabs, the setting rows and the `⋯` hit target. Several cases strip the rule they guard from the stylesheet and expect the defect, so the instrument is proved.
- `ui/filledPrimitives.browser.test.tsx` compiles the real `main.css` with Tailwind (the classes the bundle uses) and paints the filled primitives in light, dark and forced colours: the rest fill and the hover step, the focus and danger edges, the step back to the page ground inside a filled box, a badge on a hovered row, a button in a dark popover, the transparent border drawn in forced colours at no cost in size, and a note's action level with its first line. Each assertion was proved by removing the rule it guards from the compiled sheet.
- `ui/disabledPrimary.browser.test.tsx` compiles the real `main.css` the same way and paints a disabled `default` and `destructive` next to a disabled `secondary` (same ground, ink, fade and box, in light and dark), an enabled primary (still coral and danger), a working one (`aria-busy` keeps the colour, `aria-busy="false"` does not), the `aria-disabled` form, the ground in a filled card, on a recessed ground and in a popover, the variants the rule must not touch, and the system edge under forced colours. Seven mutations of the rule and the class each turn two to six of its cases red.
- `ui/dialogFromMenu.browser.test.tsx` bundles the real menus and dialogs and drives them with Playwright's keyboard and mouse: where focus lands when a dialog that a menu item opened closes, and whether its first stop wears a ring after a mouse pick.
- `ui/menuPointerRing.browser.test.tsx` drives a dropdown menu, a submenu and a context menu with the real mouse and keys under the compiled `main.css`: no ring on a row the pointer crosses, the ring back on the first key, kept for a keyboard-opened menu and for `prefers-contrast: more` and forced colours. Its first case runs without the rule and sees the ring.
- `ui/groundsAndRows.browser.test.tsx` compiles `main.css` the same way and measures the grounds table above on a page, in a card, on a recessed ground and in a card on it, a button, a neutral badge (with its painted contrast against the wash) and the real privacy pill in a washed list or settings row, and the flat rows' divider in light and forced colours. `ui/privacyPill.test.ts` holds the pill's one rule off settings rows; `ui/badge.test.tsx` holds the neutral tone to the ground-reading class with no fill utility.
- `ui/settingRowStability.browser.test.tsx` compiles `main.css` with Tailwind and the renderer's text-size pass and drives settings rows with the real keyboard and pointer, at standard and Large text: a Tab onto an InfoTip changes no pixel of the label in light, dark, `prefers-contrast: more` and forced colours, and a status line leaves the clicked switch, the value and the button where they were. Its instrument cases put the old rules back and see the overlap and the 8px slide.
- `ui/filter-input.browser.test.tsx` drives the filter field with the real pointer and keys: the hint on hover over a field the person is not in (the instrument case), and none after a click, a leave and a return, on a Tab, under a placeholder that carries the shortcut, or with ⌘F off. `ui/Select.escape.test.tsx` holds Escape to the open menu inside a dialog, and leaves a closed Select's Escape and another combobox's to the dialog. `ui/selectFocusAfterPick.browser.test.tsx` picks by key and by pointer in the real `ModalShell` and reads where focus lands; its instrument is the same Select with the blur passed, which reproduces the finding. `ui/Select.focusAfterPick.test.tsx` is the jsdom half.
- `ui/diagnosticsDialog.browser.test.tsx` bundles the real "Report a problem" dialog (daemon client stubbed, the private-chat answer released by the test), compiles `main.css` with Tailwind and the text-size pass, and measures the top edge before and after the private warning arrives, the header rule, the title at the gutter with no tile, and the footer's 32px buttons on one row at standard and Large text. Point `DIAGNOSTICS_SOURCE` at another version of the component to see the cases fail on it.
- `ui/modalEmphasisWeight.browser.test.tsx` bundles the real workflow warning and update dialogs, compiles `main.css` with Tailwind, and reads the computed weight of each emphasis (600) and of everything else in the dialog (nothing above 600), in light and dark. Its first case reads 700 from a bare `<strong>` on the same page, so a stylesheet without preflight cannot pass the rest. Point `MODAL_EMPHASIS_SOURCE_DIR` at another version of the two components to see the cases fail on it.
- `privacy/nonPrivateModelDisclosure.browser.test.tsx` bundles the real non-private model disclosure with a fixture of the served copy's length (never its words), compiles `main.css` with Tailwind and the text-size pass, and measures the top edge, the 640px width, the rules and gutters, focus on the prose with no ring, PageDown scrolling the body from the prose at the app's minimum window and the largest text size, and the "not saved" notice in view above "Continue" there. Point `DISCLOSURE_SOURCE` at another version of the component to see the cases fail on it; the raw dialog it replaced fails four.
- `styles/crewParity.test.ts` holds each promoted recipe to its Crew original, with the differences the redesign made on purpose written down.
- `ui/controlsSourceGuard.test.ts` fails on a hand-rolled checkbox, radio or switch, an `@radix-ui/themes` import, a `title=` on a menu item and a Switch `variant` outside `ui/`. The `variant` prop itself is gone, so the type checker refuses it too. A file waiting on its owner is listed in `PENDING`, and the list only shrinks; it is empty now.

To look at the components under the whole stylesheet, compile `main.css` with Vite (`server.transformRequest('/src/styles/main.css?direct')` on a middleware-mode server that loads `vite.renderer.config.mts`), bundle a small page of components with esbuild, and drive it with Playwright's Chromium using real mouse and keyboard input. `element.click()` and `focus()` from a script do not reproduce focus rings, which depend on how the focus arrived.
